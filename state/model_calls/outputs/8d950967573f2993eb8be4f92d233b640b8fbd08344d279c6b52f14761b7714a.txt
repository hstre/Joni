Literature reviews systematically synthesize existing knowledge to provide comprehensive analyses and identify specific features within a topic, such as the legal foundations in reproductive health. They establish background and significance, as seen with postpartum depression epidemiology, and they summarize prior work to motivate novel methods, exemplified by the brief review of Hochreiter’s analysis preceding a new gradient-based approach. Reviews also address gaps where comprehensive summaries are missing, critically examining method biases to recommend remedies.
